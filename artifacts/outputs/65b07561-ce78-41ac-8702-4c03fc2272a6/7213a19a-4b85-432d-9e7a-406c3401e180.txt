To compare and contrast renewable energy sources, one must examine them through three primary lenses: their **physical mechanism** (how they generate power), their **reliability/predictability** (intermittency), and their **environmental/spatial footprint**.

While all renewable sources share the common goal of replacing fossil fuels with carbon-neutral alternatives, they differ significantly in operational characteristics.

### 1. Solar Energy (Photovoltaic and Thermal)
*   **Mechanism:** Converts sunlight directly into electricity (PV) or uses heat to create steam to drive turbines (Thermal).
*   **Strengths:** Highly scalable (from residential rooftops to massive utility farms); low maintenance costs; silent operation.
*   **Weaknesses:** Intermittent (diurnal cycle and weather-dependent); requires significant land area or roof space; relies heavily on battery storage for night-time use.
*   **Contrast:** Unlike wind or hydro, solar is a "solid-state" technology (in PV), meaning it has no moving parts, leading to less mechanical wear and tear.
