Quantum entanglement is a physical phenomenon that occurs when pairs or groups of particles are generated, interact, or share spatial proximity in such a way that the quantum state of each particle cannot be described independently of the others. Even when separated by vast distances, the state of one particle instantaneously correlates with the state of its partner.

### The Core Concept: Non-Locality
In classical physics, objects are "local," meaning they can only be influenced by their immediate surroundings. In quantum mechanics, entanglement defies this. If two electrons are entangled in a state where their spins must be opposite, measuring one electron as "spin up" forces the other to become "spin down" immediately, regardless of whether the second electron is in the same room or on the other side of the galaxy.

Albert Einstein famously referred to this as **"spooky action at a distance."** However, it is important to note that entanglement does not allow for faster-than-light communication (violating the theory of relativity) because the outcome of the measurement is random; you cannot "force" a specific state to send a specific message.

### Implications for Future Computing
Quantum entanglement is the "engine" behind quantum computing, providing capabilities that classical binary systems cannot replicate.

#### 1. Exponential Processing Power (Superposition + Entanglement)
While **superposition** allows a quantum bit (qubit) to exist in multiple states simultaneously (0 and 1), **entanglement** allows these qubits to work in unison. When qubits are entangled, the state of the entire system is a complex web of correlations. Adding a single qubit to an entangled system doubles the computational space. This allows a quantum computer to process a massive number of possibilities at once, making it uniquely suited for:
*   **Prime Factorization:** Breaking modern encryption codes.
*   **Molecular Simulation:** Modeling complex chemical reactions for drug discovery that are too complex for classical supercomputers.
